# Write from the user's executive perspective

The requested voice is a CTO/CEO addressing a team, peers or their future self. Deliver a document ready to share, not an assistant explaining its process to the user. Use the requested language; repository guidance remains English.

## Lead with what matters

Open with the conclusion, decision or unresolved question. Follow with facts, their implications and the next action. Be direct and pragmatic. Completeness comes from evidence and useful detail, not from generic introductions or repeated summaries.

Distinguish four levels: observed fact, transparent calculation, hypothesis and proposal. Cite sources and periods. Label synthetic demonstrations. Do not invent first-person experiences, ownership, commitments, dates, metrics or agreement. If the data is missing, name the gap and the decision it blocks.

## Executive report structure

1. The result or decision to make, with scope and date.
2. Highlights: meaningful progress with observable evidence.
3. Lowlights: constraints, regressions, uncertainty and consequences.
4. Evidence: comparable data, sources, denominators and methods.
5. Alternatives and tradeoffs, including conditions that would change the recommendation.
6. Actions with known owners, dates and success criteria; explicitly leave unknown assignments undecided.

Adapt this structure for articles, runbooks and personal notes. Do not force highlights/lowlights onto every paragraph. Separate observed business outcomes from shipped capabilities: an installed skill does not prove adoption, a feature does not prove time saved, and a chart does not prove causality.

## Component choices

Use a decision record for the recommendation, outlined cards for genuinely parallel highlights, a table for evidence, an attention map for parts of a valid whole and charts for scaled comparisons. Put methodological details in disclosures only after stating the main limitation. Marginal handwriting can ask a pointed question about a nearby claim. Timelines show actual changes, not invented progress. Prototype comparisons make behavioral differences inspectable.

Use the greatest useful variety without ornamental overload. Preserve exact values, units, source dates and missing data. Shared comments and private notes feed an explicit context bundle for the next revision; they do not become public by default.

## Editing checklist

Can a reader state the decision after the opening paragraph? Can they distinguish measured outcomes from proposals? Are sources and limits nearby? Does each section add evidence, explanation or action? Are ownership and timing real? Are terms familiar to the intended team? Remove empty praise, corporate filler, vague claims of transformation and repeated conclusions.

The reference books inform independent principles, not copied prose or impersonation. [Communication references](communication-references.md) describes what was actually consulted. Do not claim full-book reading without access to the full text.
